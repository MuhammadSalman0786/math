About All Math Solver Step-by-Step Math & Induction Solver

Comprehensive BODMAS Execution & Formal Mathematical Proofs

The All Math Solver Expression Solver is built to resolve everyday arithmetic and advanced mathematical proofs without skipping a single detail. Operating under strict order of operations (Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication & Division, Addition & Subtraction), each calculation is performed one line at a time to ensure complete conceptual clarity for students and researchers.

For high school, A-level and college students, the solver incorporates an advanced Mathematical Induction Engine. When propositions such as \(1^3 + 2^3 + \dots + n^3 = \frac{n^2(n+1)^2}{4}\) or sum of squares are entered, the engine systematically verifies the Base Step (\(n = 1\)), establishes the Inductive Hypothesis (\(n = k\)), executes algebraic LCM factoring for \(n = k + 1\), and presents a rigorous textbook conclusion.

About the Mathematical Induction Solver

Base case, induction hypothesis and inductive step, written out in full

Mathematical induction proves that a statement \(P(n)\) is true for every whole number \(n\). You show that \(P(1)\) is true (the base case), assume \(P(k)\) is true (the induction hypothesis), and then prove \(P(k+1)\) from it (the inductive step).

This induction solver writes the whole proof for sum formulas such as \(1 + 3 + 5 + \dots + (2n-1) = n^2\) or \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} i^3 = \frac{n^2(n+1)^2}{4}\), geometric sums such as \(1 + 2 + 4 + \dots + 2^{n-1} = 2^n - 1\), telescoping fractions, and divisibility statements such as "\(4^n - 1\) is divisible by 3". If a formula is wrong, it tells you which value of \(n\) fails.

How the Quadratic Equation Calculator Works

Quadratic formula \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\), discriminant and factorising

A quadratic equation has the form \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) with \(a \neq 0\). The calculator first works out the discriminant \(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\): if \(\Delta > 0\) there are two real roots, if \(\Delta = 0\) there is one repeated root, and if \(\Delta < 0\) the roots are complex.

It then factorises the quadratic when it has rational roots, applies the quadratic formula, and gives exact answers with surds (for example \(\frac{3 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}\)) as well as decimals. It also shows the sum and product of the roots and the vertex of the parabola.

How to Find the LCM (Least Common Multiple)

Prime factorisation method, checked with Euclid's algorithm

The least common multiple of two or more numbers is the smallest positive number that every one of them divides into exactly. For example, the LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.

This LCM calculator writes each number as a product of primes and takes every prime with its highest power: \(12 = 2^2 \times 3\) and \(18 = 2 \times 3^2\) give \(\text{LCM} = 2^2 \times 3^2 = 36\). It also checks the answer with \(\text{LCM}(a,b) = \frac{a \times b}{\text{GCF}(a,b)}\). The LCM is used to add fractions with different denominators.

How to Find the GCF, HCF or GCD

Greatest common factor by prime factors and Euclid's algorithm

The greatest common factor (GCF) is the largest number that divides all the given numbers exactly. It is also called the highest common factor (HCF) in the UK and India, and the greatest common divisor (GCD) in many textbooks.

The calculator takes only the primes common to every number, with their lowest power: \(48 = 2^4 \times 3\) and \(180 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5\) give \(\text{GCF} = 2^2 \times 3 = 12\). It confirms the result with Euclid's algorithm, the quickest method for large numbers. The GCF is used to simplify fractions.

Percentage Formulas Explained

Percent of a number, percentage of a total, and percentage increase or decrease

"Per cent" means "out of 100", so \(15\%\) is \(\frac{15}{100} = 0.15\). To find X% of Y, multiply: \(15\% \text{ of } 80 = 0.15 \times 80 = 12\). To find what percentage one number is of another, divide and multiply by 100: \(\frac{30}{120} \times 100 = 25\%\).

Percentage change is \(\frac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}} \times 100\%\): going from 50 to 65 is a 30% increase. Useful for discounts and sales, VAT and sales tax, pay rises, marks and exam scores, and interest.

How Proportion & Cross-Multiplication Works

Solving Unknown Variables in Rational Fractions \(\frac{A}{B} = \frac{C}{D}\)

A mathematical proportion is an equation stating that two distinct ratios or fractions are equivalent. In standard form, given \(\frac{A}{B} = \frac{C}{D}\), the fundamental property of cross-products dictates that the product of extremes equals the product of means: \(A \times D = B \times C\).

Our Proportion Calculator and proportion solver allows you to place an unknown variable (such as \(x\)) in any of the four numerator or denominator fields. It instantly generates the isolated cross product, performs right-hand numeric multiplication, isolates the variable through division, and yields accurate 4-decimal precision.

Chronological Age Calculation & Time Metrics

Accurate Gregorian Calendar Days, Leap Year Handling, & Living Hours

The All Math Solver Age Calculator determines the exact elapsed duration between a person's date of birth and the current calendar day. Unlike rudimentary approximations that divide elapsed days by 365, our algorithm dynamically accommodates varying month lengths alongside leap year compensations.

In addition to rendering exact years, elapsed months, and residual days, the breakdown presents total living days and cumulative living hours.

AC to DC Conversion & Rectification Fundamentals

Peak Voltage, Root Mean Square (RMS), & DC Output Characteristics

Alternating Current (AC) oscillates periodically in a sinusoidal waveform. For a Full-Wave Bridge Rectifier, both polarities of the AC cycle are directed to the load, resulting in an average DC voltage of \(V_{DC} = \frac{2 \times V_{peak}}{\pi} \approx 0.6366 \times V_{peak}\). In contrast, a Half-Wave Rectifier utilizes only one half of the waveform, delivering \(V_{DC} = \frac{V_{peak}}{\pi} \approx 0.3183 \times V_{peak}\).

What are cookies?

Cookies are small text files that a website stores in your browser. Similar technologies, such as your browser's local storage, keep small amounts of data on your device. This policy explains what All Math Solver stores and why.

What we store

  • Sign-in session (essential): if you log in, a secure session token keeps you signed in. It is removed when you log out.
  • Recent history and settings (functional): your recent calculations and preferences such as light or dark mode are kept in your browser's local storage so they are still there on your next visit.
  • Language choice (functional): when you pick a language other than English, a cookie named googtrans remembers it, and Google Translate is loaded to translate the page. Google may set its own cookies when it is used.
  • Visit statistics (analytics): we use Google Analytics 4, loaded through Google Tag Manager, to count visits and see which pages are used. It sets the cookies _ga and _ga_<ID>, which keep a random ID so that repeat visits can be counted. They last up to 2 years.

Analytics: what it tells us

Google Analytics 4 shows us totals, such as how many people visited, which pages and calculators they used, roughly which country they are in, and what kind of device they used. It does not tell us your name or email address. Google Analytics 4 does not store IP addresses. We use these numbers only to improve the site.

We also use Google Search Console, a free Google tool for website owners. It shows how our pages appear in Google Search, for example which search terms brought visitors. It does not set any cookies on our site.

Advertising

We do not use advertising cookies, and we do not sell your data.

How to control cookies

You can delete cookies and local storage at any time in your browser settings, and you can block cookies completely. If you block essential cookies, signing in will not work, but the calculators will still work. Choosing English in the language menu removes the language cookie.

To stop Google Analytics on all websites, you can install Google's Analytics opt-out browser add-on, or block cookies from google-analytics.com in your browser. The site keeps working normally.
